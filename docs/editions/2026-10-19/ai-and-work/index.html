Anthropic sets out a fund that could pay people displaced by AI

The 5% share pledge would sit in a company-owned LLC, needs stockholder approval, and could make direct payments to groups hit by job disruption.

The Signal · Monday 19 October 2026

Key facts

  • Anthropic is pledging 5% of its shares to a public benefit fund held in a company-owned LLC.
  • The board could add 2% more each time the company's value rises 50% above a baseline.
  • The fund could make direct payments to groups hit by job disruption, but may not fund politics or lobbying.
  • The plan needs stockholder approval; about 20% of shares are now earmarked for public benefit.

The plan

Anthropic says it will put 5% of its shares into a fund for public benefit, held in a limited liability company it controls. The pledge needs stockholder approval. The board could add a further 2% each time the company's value rises 50% above a baseline it has not disclosed.

The fund may give large grants or make direct payments to specific groups, and Anthropic names people affected by labor-market disruption as an example. It may not fund political activity or lobbying. Counting earlier pledges by its cofounders and employees, about 20% of the company's shares are now earmarked for public benefit, Forbes reports. Governance details are due in the coming months.

Context

Anthropic is organised as a public benefit corporation, a Delaware structure that requires directors to weigh the company's stated public mission and the people affected by its conduct alongside shareholders' interests. The company is preparing for what could be one of the largest stock market listings on record.

The plan has critics. David Sacks, who co-chairs the president's science council, questioned whether such a vehicle serves the public.

Why it matters

An AI lab is pre-committing part of its own upside to the people its products may put out of work. Whether it pays out at scale will be an early test of who captures AI's gains.
