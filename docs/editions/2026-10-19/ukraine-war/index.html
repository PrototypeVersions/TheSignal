DevelopingRussia–Ukraine war

Russia strikes back at Ukraine's grid, cutting power to 550,000 homes

A day after Ukraine's 1,000-drone raid on Russia, Moscow fired about 270 drones and a barrage of missiles overnight, hitting energy sites in 11 regions, Zelenskyy says.

The Signal · Monday 19 October 2026 · Updated 6:05pm CT

Key facts

  • Russia launched 269 drones, 59 of them jet-powered, plus Iskander ballistic and cruise missiles overnight, Ukraine's air force says.
  • About 550,000 households lost power, and energy sites were hit in 11 regions.
  • Russian attacks over the past day killed three people and injured 53 across Ukraine.
  • The US is taking up to 60 days to review the Russia sanctions it waived on Friday.

The overnight strike

Russia answered Ukraine's weekend raid with one of its own. Ukraine's air force counted 269 drones, 59 of them jet-powered, along with Iskander ballistic missiles and cruise missiles. It says air defences brought down 243 drones and four missiles, but that strikes landed at 25 locations.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said 16 ballistic missiles were aimed mainly at the Kyiv region and that more than ten energy facilities were damaged, with energy sites hit in 11 regions in all. About 550,000 households were left without power. In Kyiv one person was killed and eight were injured, according to AP; across Ukraine, Russian attacks over the past day killed three people and injured 53, the Kyiv Independent reports. Russia's Defence Ministry says it struck a logistics hub, a data center and a cargo ship off Odesa.

A war on both grids

The strike came a day after Ukraine's largest attack on the St Petersburg region in four years, by the count of Russia's TASS news agency. Ukrainian drones hit the St Petersburg oil terminal and the Ruchi oil depot, and about 20,000 people in the Leningrad region lost power; Moscow said it brought down 1,015 Ukrainian drones overnight.

Zelenskyy says Russia has launched almost 2,700 attack drones, 1,700 aerial bombs and 66 missiles at Ukraine in the past week, and has struck Ukraine's energy and oil and gas sectors 76 times since President Trump announced an energy ceasefire a week earlier. With the heating season starting, both sides are now aiming at the systems that keep the other's homes warm.

Washington's pause

US pressure on Moscow is on hold. Secretary of State Marco Rubio says Trump will take up to 60 days to study the impact of the sanctions he waived on Friday, and Congress is in recess until after the 3 November midterms, so there is no quick route to override him. Zelenskyy has criticised the waiver, saying it will encourage further attacks.

Two other threads matter for Kyiv. Zelenskyy told NBC that US intelligence sharing, blocked for a period, has resumed. And Rubio said on Saturday that Ukraine has asked for access to Starshield, the military version of SpaceX's Starlink, for deep strikes inside Russia; it does not have access "at this moment", he said, and the request is under discussion.

What comes next

The first test is this week's power-cut schedules in Ukraine, which will show how much of the damage can be repaired quickly. Beyond that, watch whether Ukraine hits Russian refineries or ports again, and whether a reported Putin proposal for a summit with Trump and Xi Jinping next month takes shape.

Why it matters

Both sides are now hitting each other's energy systems at a scale of hundreds of drones a night as the heating season begins, while the main US pressure on Moscow is paused for up to two months. Nothing on the table is slowing the energy war.

What to watch

Ukrainian power-cut schedules this week; whether Ukraine strikes Russian refineries or ports again; a reported Putin proposal for a summit with Trump and Xi next month.

Since this morning

  • Update, 6:05pm CT

    Zelenskyy says Ukraine got no early warning from partner countries before the overnight strike, which damaged 28 substations, and that Patriot air defence is in short supply. US and Russian officials say talks continue on a Trump–Putin–Xi meeting at next month's APEC summit in China without Ukraine; Kyiv rejects the idea, and the Kremlin says no substantive proposals have been made.
