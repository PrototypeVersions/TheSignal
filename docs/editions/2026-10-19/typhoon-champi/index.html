Super Typhoon Champi heads for the Philippines and Taiwan after skirting Guam

The storm passed about 10 miles from Guam, cutting power to about half the island, then briefly reached super typhoon strength.

The Signal · Monday 19 October 2026 · Updated 6:05pm CT

Since this morning

  • Update, 6:05pm CT

    Champi regained super typhoon strength on Monday afternoon, with winds of 185 km/h, and is due to enter the Philippine area on Tuesday as Rosal. Taiwan's weather agency now expects it to turn north earlier, near Japan's Ishigaki Island, sparing Taiwan a direct hit but bringing heavy rain to the north on Friday and Saturday.

Guam and the Northern Marianas

Champi passed about 10 miles from Guam, cutting power to about half the island, and briefly reached super typhoon strength after it moved on. President Trump approved an emergency declaration for Guam on Saturday.

In the Northern Mariana Islands, Tinian took more than two feet of rain, and a broken waterline left about 70% of residents without water; officials hoped to fix the pipes by Tuesday. Saipan saw winds of up to 70 mph and more than 11 inches of rain, and more than 300 people sheltered in schools and community centres as the storm passed.

Heading west

Philippine forecasters expect Champi to enter the country's area of responsibility on Tuesday, where it will be named Rosal, and say it may re-intensify into a super typhoon; northern Luzon could see wind warnings. Taiwan's weather agency says it could issue a sea warning as early as Wednesday night, with the heaviest impact on Friday and Saturday, though forecast models still disagree on whether the storm turns north before reaching the island.

Why it matters

A storm of this strength on a track toward northern Luzon and Taiwan threatens dense coasts and key shipping and chip-making regions late this week.

What to watch

Whether Champi turns north on Tuesday; Taiwan's warnings from Wednesday night.
