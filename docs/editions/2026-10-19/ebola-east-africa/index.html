Congo's Ebola outbreak reaches an eighth province

Two cases, one fatal, in Nord-Ubangi take the outbreak past 9,400 cases and 4,500 deaths. Kenya's eighth case was already in quarantine.

The Signal · Monday 19 October 2026

Key facts

  • Congo's outbreak has passed 9,400 cases and 4,500 deaths, the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record.
  • Nord-Ubangi, which borders the Central African Republic, is the eighth province affected.
  • The Bundibugyo strain has no approved vaccine or treatment.
  • Kenya has eight cases, all linked to one imported case; 128 of 130 contacts are in quarantine.

An eighth province

Two cases, one of them fatal, have been confirmed in Nord-Ubangi, making it the eighth Congolese province to report Ebola. The province borders the Central African Republic. The outbreak, declared in mid-May, has now passed 9,400 cases and 4,500 deaths across 66 health zones, making it the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record. About three-quarters of confirmed cases are in Ituri, the epicentre, AP reports; cases are also confirmed in North and South Kivu, Tshopo, Haut-Uele, Bas-Uele and Sud-Ubangi.

Fewer hotspots, a wider map

The WHO's Africa director, Mohamed Janabi, said pockets of infection are generally declining but the disease is spreading geographically. That combination matters: each new province means more borders, roads and trade routes to watch. The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo strain, for which there is no approved vaccine or treatment; several candidate vaccines are in early human trials. Uganda, which had cases earlier, has been declared Ebola-free.

Kenya's cluster

Kenya's eighth case is a 49-year-old attendant who cared for the country's first patient and tested positive while already in quarantine; 38 people were tested and only he was positive, health officials said. The first patient had been ill in Congo for about a month before flying to Nairobi on 3 October, and died on 5 October.

Officials say 128 of his 130 known contacts are in quarantine, above the 85% benchmark usually needed to break chains of transmission, and that there is still no evidence of community spread. Kenya has stepped up screening and opened a hotline for anyone who may have been exposed.

What comes next

The key signal in Kenya is any case outside the known contact list, which would mean the virus is spreading in the community. In the region, watch for new provinces or countries reporting cases, particularly along the border with the Central African Republic.

Why it matters

Fewer hotspots but a wider map means more borders and supply routes exposed. Kenya's cluster still looks contained, which is what keeps this regional rather than continental.

What to watch

Any Kenyan case outside the contact list; new provinces or countries reporting cases.
