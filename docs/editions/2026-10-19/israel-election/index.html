Edition 2, Monday 19 October 2026 / Israel's election

NewIntensifyingIsrael's election

Israel's election is deadlocked, and Trump endorses Netanyahu

Four polls published Sunday leave neither bloc near the 61 seats needed to govern, eight days before the 27 October vote.

The Signal · Monday 19 October 2026

Key facts

  • Four polls published Sunday give Netanyahu's bloc 49 to 53 seats and the opposition 50 to 54; 61 of 120 are needed to govern.
  • Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party averages 21.5 seats against 19 for Likud.
  • President Trump has endorsed Netanyahu.
  • Israelis vote on 27 October.

A hung Knesset in the polls

Eight days before the vote, neither side is near a majority. Four polls published on Sunday, compiled by the Jerusalem Post, give Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc between 49 and 53 of the Knesset's 120 seats and the opposition bloc between 50 and 54. A government needs 61. Former army chief Gadi Eisenkot's centrist Yashar party averages 21.5 seats, ahead of Netanyahu's Likud on 19.

The campaign

President Trump has endorsed Netanyahu, who has set out a security doctrine built on pre-emptive strikes and buffer zones. The governing side campaigns on degrading Hamas and weakening Hezbollah and Iran; the opposition promises lower living costs, an inquiry into the failures around 7 October and drafting ultra-Orthodox men into the army. Eisenkot, Netanyahu's main challenger, also opposes a Palestinian state.

Israel's growing diplomatic isolation is barely discussed. Netanyahu is wanted by the International Criminal Court, and analysts expect a Netanyahu win to deepen the rift with Europe.

The West Bank in the background

The campaign runs alongside a crisis in the occupied West Bank. On 17 October masked settlers attacked a delegation of French, Belgian and Polish diplomats near the village of Taybeh, and a Washington Post photographer was seriously injured in another attack. The EU has demanded "full accountability". There were still no arrests on Sunday, Netanyahu said there was no compelling evidence that settlers attacked the diplomats, and US officials visited Taybeh.

What comes next

Watch the final polls this week, turnout on 27 October, and whether parties outside both blocs become kingmakers. A close result would mean weeks of coalition bargaining in the middle of a regional war.

Why it matters

The next government will shape Israel's part in the regional war and its West Bank policy. Polls point to a hung Knesset and weeks of coalition bargaining in wartime.

What to watch

The last polls this week; turnout on 27 October; whether parties outside both blocs become kingmakers.
