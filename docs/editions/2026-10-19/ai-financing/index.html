Chip stocks suffer their longest slide since 2008 as AI debt gets dearer

The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 11.4% last week, and the cloud provider CoreWeave dropped 12% on Friday alone.

The Signal · Monday 19 October 2026

Key facts

  • The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 11.4% last week.
  • CoreWeave, a cloud provider built on AI chips, dropped 12% on Friday alone.
  • Anthropic's $60 billion chip financing pays Blackstone a 10% coupon on $9 billion of junior debt, against about 7% for similar borrowers.
  • Microsoft has walked away as anchor tenant of Nscale's planned UK data center.

The selloff

Chip stocks are on their longest slide since 2008. The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 11.4% last week, and CoreWeave, a cloud provider whose business is renting out AI chips, dropped 12% on Friday alone.

The price of money

New details of Anthropic's chip financing, first reported by the Financial Times, show what lenders now want. The $60 billion package pays for about 1.5 gigawatts of Google's TPU chips, with roughly $42 billion of senior debt guaranteed by Broadcom and $18 billion of junior debt. Blackstone is expected to take $9 billion of the junior slice at a 10% coupon, while borrowers with similar ratings pay around 7%. The rating agency KBRA rates Anthropic one notch below investment grade.

There are signs of strain elsewhere in the build-out. Microsoft has walked away as anchor tenant of Nscale's planned site in the UK, which may not be connected to the grid until the 2030s.

Why it matters

Investors are starting to charge for the risk in the AI build-out rather than just fund it. If financing costs keep rising while chip stocks fall, the pace of new data centers will be set by credit markets as much as by demand.
