Alberta votes on separation after 1.3 million ballots cast early

Elections Alberta says 43.1% of eligible voters had already voted before Monday. Polls close at 8pm Mountain time (9pm Central).

The Signal · Monday 19 October 2026

Key facts

  • More than 1.3 million Albertans, 43.1% of eligible voters, voted before Monday, Elections Alberta says.
  • The question asks whether Alberta should stay in Canada or start the process toward a binding vote on separation.
  • The final Angus Reid poll had 66% planning to vote to stay.
  • Polls close at 8pm Mountain time (9pm Central).

The vote

Albertans finish voting today on whether their province should remain in Canada or have the provincial government begin the legal process toward a binding referendum on separation. It is one of ten questions on the ballot. Heavy early voting points to the high turnout officials expected: Elections Alberta says more than 1.3 million people, 43.1% of eligible voters, had already voted before Monday. Polls close at 8pm Mountain time, 9pm Central.

How Alberta got here

In 2025 Premier Danielle Smith's government lowered the threshold for citizen-led referendums. A pro-Canada petition gathered more than 400,000 verified signatures, and a separatist petition about 223,000 after a court-ordered check. In May a court struck down the separatist petition for failing to consult Indigenous peoples properly, and Smith then put a single remain-or-separate question on the October ballot.

A win for the separation option would not take Alberta out of Canada. The government would have to begin the process for a second, binding referendum, and Canada's constitution has no route for a province to leave on its own; any separation would need a constitutional amendment under the Clarity Act framework.

What the polls say

The final Angus Reid poll had 66% planning to vote to stay a province, and The Writ's average, as of Sunday, put remain at 68% and the separation option at 26%. Among people who would back a binding separation vote, 60% told Angus Reid they mainly wanted to send Ottawa a message, while 37% said they want an independent country.

Smith has said she would seek a binding referendum if the separation option wins a majority. Prime Minister Mark Carney campaigned for staying in Canada, as did federal Conservative leader Pierre Poilievre and former premiers Rachel Notley and Jason Kenney.

What comes next

Results start from 9pm Central tonight. The number to watch is the separation vote against the polls' roughly 26 to 34%: a clear defeat would blunt the movement, while a closer result would keep it alive into Alberta's 2027 election.
