DevelopingFrance unrest

France has a calm night under its first curfews as Macron tours the Gulf

Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said targeted curfews in Paris, Lille, Nice and Lyon helped bring a quiet night, while critics accused Macron of being away during the crisis.

The Signal · Tuesday 20 October 2026

Key facts

  • Paris imposed an 11pm–6am curfew around Place de la République and Les Halles on Monday night and banned gatherings citywide from 8pm.
  • Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez says 2,513 people have been taken into custody and 67 jailed since the unrest began.
  • In Milan, more than 200 people have been arrested since Saturday after two days of clashes.
  • Macron is on a Gulf tour from 19 to 21 October seeking oil and diesel supplies.

The first night of curfews

France had its first quiet night since the weekend's rioting. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu gave mayors and prefects power on Monday to impose local curfews, saying a curfew for all of Paris was out of the question and that a state of emergency was possible but not yet needed. Paris barred people from the areas around Place de la République and Les Halles from 11pm to 6am and banned gatherings across the city from 8pm. Lille and nearby towns, Nice and Lyon imposed curfews, some for minors only.

On Tuesday morning Lecornu wrote that the targeted curfews had contributed to the calm of the night, and said prefects and mayors would set more where needed, ABC News reports. No overnight arrest figure has been published.

The toll so far

Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said on Monday that 2,513 people had been taken into custody and 67 jailed. Almost 1,000 police and gendarmes were injured on Saturday alone, and at least 3,000 businesses have been damaged or looted, AP reports. Nuñez offered to resign on Sunday, saying police had lost control; Macron refused and ordered an account of planning and intelligence failures.

The trouble has spread beyond France. In Milan, more than 200 people have been arrested since Saturday after clashes around the cathedral square, with similar trouble in Bologna and Turin, Euronews reports. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said protest is a right but laying waste to cities is not.

Macron abroad

President Emmanuel Macron left on Monday for a tour of Kuwait, the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain and Qatar, running to 21 October, with energy executives, to secure oil and diesel supplies disrupted by the Iran war, The New Arab reports. Former prime ministers Édouard Philippe and Gabriel Attal say he has lost authority and responded too late, and the far-right leader Jordan Bardella said the government was collapsing, according to Euronews.

What comes next

Curfews for minors in the Lille area run through Wednesday night. Al Jazeera reports, citing France 24, that protesters have called a new round of demonstrations for Saturday 24 October.

Why it matters

The curfews appear to have broken the cycle of nightly rioting for now, but the political cost is landing on a president whose term ends next spring, ahead of an April 2027 election in which the far right leads in polls.

What to watch

Whether calm holds through Wednesday; Macron's return after 21 October; Saturday's planned protests; whether Italy's unrest grows.
