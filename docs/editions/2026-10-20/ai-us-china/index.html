US orders DeepSeek out of federal systems, including tools built on its models

A Homeland Security order made public on Monday bars the Chinese lab's products from civilian agencies and requires removal of third-party products built on them.

The Signal · Tuesday 20 October 2026

Key facts

  • The order, under the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act of 2018, was filed for public inspection on 19 October.
  • It covers DeepSeek products and third-party products built using DeepSeek implementations; national security systems are excluded.
  • It takes effect immediately; agencies have 60 days to ask in writing for an exception.
  • DeepSeek released an updated open-weights model, V4.1-Flash, under the MIT licence on 17 October.

The order

The Department of Homeland Security has ordered federal civilian agencies to stop buying and to remove products and services from Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence and its affiliates, IBTimes reports. The exclusion and removal order, dated 24 July, was filed for public inspection on Monday 19 October under the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act of 2018, on the recommendation of the Federal Acquisition Security Council.

Its reach goes beyond DeepSeek's own apps. It also requires removal of third-party products built using DeepSeek implementations, which could cover vendors that embed its openly released models. National security systems are excluded because they fall under separate rules. The order takes effect immediately, and agencies have 60 days to request an exception in writing.

The reasons given

DHS cites DeepSeek's base in China and its servers there, Chinese laws that allow state access to company data, the collection of personal data, the risk that prompts could send sensitive information to China, and research that found serious flaws in its iPhone app. DeepSeek did not comment, and the Chinese Embassy did not immediately respond. The Commerce Department, the Navy and several states had already restricted the company's apps on government devices.

What comes next

The order arrived days after DeepSeek released V4.1-Flash, an updated model with open weights under the permissive MIT licence that it says scores markedly higher on agentic coding tests. The practical question now is how agencies and contractors read the clause on products built on DeepSeek, since its open models can be downloaded and run without any connection to the company.

Why it matters

The order reaches past a Chinese AI company's own apps to the products built on its open models. How widely that clause is applied will shape whether US contractors can use Chinese open-weights models at all.
