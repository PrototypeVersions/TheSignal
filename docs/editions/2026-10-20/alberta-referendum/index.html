Edition 3, Tuesday 20 October 2026

Major shiftAlberta referendum

Albertans reject a path to separation by about three to one

About 76% voted to stay in Canada rather than start a process toward a binding vote on leaving, CTV and CBC projected, a far wider margin than separatists had hoped for.

The Signal · Tuesday 20 October 2026

Key facts

  • On question 10, about 76% chose to remain in Canada and about 24% backed a process toward a binding separation vote, by early counts.
  • More than 1.35 million ballots were cast in advance or by mail, against about 810,000 that way in the 2023 provincial election.
  • Central Peace-Notley was the only riding where a majority backed a second referendum, with 51.1% at last count.
  • Results for the nine other questions are partial; official results are due on Wednesday 21 October.

The result

Albertans voted on Monday to stay in Canada, and by a wide margin. On the tenth and last question on the ballot, which asked whether the province should begin the legal process toward a binding referendum on leaving the country, about 76% chose to remain, CTV News and CBC projected on the night. CTV's live count early on Tuesday stood at 76.4% to 23.6%; Al Jazeera, with more than a million votes counted, put it at about 77% to 23%.

The vote was not close anywhere in the big cities. No riding in Edmonton or Calgary fell below 75% for staying, according to CTV's count. Many rural ridings in the north, centre and south were in the high 50s or low 60s for remaining, and only one, Central Peace-Notley in the northwest, had a majority for a second referendum, at 51.1%.

Turnout and the other questions

Interest was high. More than 1.35 million ballots were cast in advance polls or by special mail-in ballot, Global News reports, compared with about 810,000 cast those ways in the 2023 provincial election; about 519,000 mail-in ballots alone arrived before the cut-off. More than 100 polling stations opened late, delaying results by over three hours. No official turnout figure has been published yet.

The other nine questions, on immigration, social supports and the constitution, were counted after the separation question and remain partial. Early figures show questions on more provincial control of immigration and a 12-month residency rule for social supports passing comfortably, while questions on choosing judges and putting provincial laws ahead of federal ones were close to tied. Official results are expected on Wednesday.

The reaction

Premier Danielle Smith, who called the vote but backed staying in Canada, said Albertans had voted for a stronger Alberta in a united Canada. Prime Minister Mark Carney said Albertans chose unity. Thomas Lukaszuk, who led the Forever Canadian campaign, said the message had been sent, while arguing that the roughly $130 million vote need not have been held.

Separatists say they are not finished. Jeffrey Rath of the Alberta Prosperity Project said the movement was not going anywhere, and he wants a special meeting of Smith's United Conservative Party to test the base's confidence in her. Before the vote his colleague Mitch Sylvestre had said support above 40% would put independence in play; it came in well short.

What comes next

Smith addresses the province on Tuesday evening at 6:45pm Mountain time. She has promised that there will be no follow-up separation referendum during her government's mandate, which runs to the election due in October 2027. A future government or a new citizen petition could still bring the question back.

Why it matters

The largest test of separatist feeling in Western Canada since the movement revived has produced a decisive no. It takes a formal route out of Canada off the table for now, though the closer results in rural ridings show the grievances behind it remain.

What to watch

Smith's televised address on Tuesday evening; official results for all ten questions on Wednesday 21 October; whether separatists force a UCP leadership review before the party's November meeting.
