DevelopingIran war and the Gulf

Oil climbs toward $112 as Iran claims tanker mine strikes and Houthis aim at Riyadh

Brent reached a five-month high on Tuesday after Iran's Revolutionary Guards said tankers hit mines in Hormuz, and Saudi Arabia intercepted a Houthi missile fired at its capital.

The Signal · Tuesday 20 October 2026

Key facts

  • Brent crude was about $111.6 a barrel early on Tuesday, its highest in roughly five months.
  • The death toll from Monday's Houthi missile strike on a Yemeni army training camp in Shabwa has risen to 60, AP reports.
  • Iran's Revolutionary Guards say tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday and Monday; the claims are not independently confirmed.
  • Russia's foreign minister, Sergey Lavrov, visited Tehran on Monday, his first trip there since the war began.

Hormuz and oil

Iran's Revolutionary Guards say two tankers struck mines and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday and another on Monday, in the southern part of the waterway. Iran says the ships were using a route endorsed by the United States that Tehran does not recognise, with their transponders off, and its naval commander said such ships will be treated as hostile. The claims have not been independently confirmed. The UK's maritime security agency says October is already the worst month for attacks on shipping in the strait since March, according to CBS News.

Early on Tuesday the Guards also said they had shot down a US MQ-9 Reaper drone over the strait; there was no US confirmation. Brent crude, which had slipped on Monday after Washington eased sanctions on Russian fuel, rose about 1.9% to around $111.6 a barrel on Tuesday, its highest level in about five months, according to Trading Economics.

The Houthi front

The death toll from Monday's Houthi ballistic missile strike on the al-Alam training camp in Shabwa province has risen to 60, most of them new recruits, with more than 200 wounded, AP reports, making it the deadliest attack in months. Provincial officials say three missiles hit recruits lining up for training. The Houthis claimed the attack.

On Tuesday Saudi Arabia said it intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh, where an explosion was reported in the north of the city near King Khalid International Airport. Egypt's parliament, meanwhile, put off until 26 October a vote that would let President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi send forces to help defend Saudi Arabia, Al Jazeera reports.

Diplomacy and the next round

Iran's offer to reopen the strait is still on the table without a US answer. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on Sunday that Tehran was reviewing US comments on a plan under which the strait would reopen on day six if the US lifts its naval blockade and oil sanctions, with talks starting on day seven. Lavrov's visit to Tehran on Monday, where Araghchi said ties had never been closer, cut against President Trump's claim that Russia is doing little to help Iran.

US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth made an unannounced visit to Israel on Monday and met Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Axios has reported, according to Al Jazeera, that Trump and Netanyahu discussed a major new operation against Iran, though Trump has said he will not attack before the 3 November midterms. The US mission for Iran urged Americans in the region to be vigilant.

Why it matters

Both chokepoints of the war, Hormuz and the Saudi–Houthi front, are getting worse at once, and oil is back near its spring peak two weeks before US elections fought partly over fuel prices.

What to watch

Pakistan's army chief in Tehran on Tuesday; any US reply to Iran's six-day plan; Egypt's parliamentary vote on 26 October; whether strikes on Iran resume around Israel's 27 October election or after 3 November.
