DevelopingIsrael's election

A week before Israel votes, Hegseth visits and the polls stay tied

The latest polls give Netanyahu's bloc and the opposition about 51 seats each, short of 61, as the US defence secretary paid an unannounced visit and Benny Gantz dropped out.

The Signal · Tuesday 20 October 2026

Key facts

  • A Jerusalem Post average of four polls gives Netanyahu's bloc 51 seats and the opposition 51.25, with Arab parties at 13.75.
  • Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar! averages 21.5 seats against 19 for Netanyahu's Likud.
  • US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth met Netanyahu in Israel on Monday, eight days before the vote.
  • Benny Gantz has dropped out; his Kahol Lavan party was polling about 1%.

The numbers

Israel's election on 27 October remains deadlocked. Four polls published at the weekend, averaged by the Jerusalem Post, give Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc 51 seats and the opposition led by former army chief Gadi Eisenkot 51.25, with Arab parties at 13.75; neither reaches the 61 needed to govern. Eisenkot's Yashar! averages 21.5 seats and Likud 19.

Individual polls vary. Channel 12 had the opposition ahead by 54 to 49, while the public broadcaster Kan's poll, reported by Haaretz, put it at 52 to 51. In Kan's poll a new far-right party led by Ofer Winter fell just below the 3.25% threshold, with Bezalel Smotrich's Religious Zionism gaining. The opposition only passes 61 with Arab parties, which parts of it refuse to work with.

Washington in the campaign

President Trump gave Netanyahu his full endorsement on Sunday, warning that if Israel picked the wrong leader the US would face Iran alone. Netanyahu's office released a call in which Trump predicted a tremendous victory. On Monday Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth made an unannounced visit and met Netanyahu. Eisenkot replied that Israelis, not Trump, choose their prime minister, TIME reports.

Analysts at Chatham House and King's College London called the endorsement significant but said its effect on votes should not be overstated. Benny Gantz, once the opposition's leading figure, has left the race.

What comes next

Israeli law bars publishing polls in the final days before the vote, so the last surveys are expected around Friday. Talk of military action against Iran before polling day hangs over the final week.

Why it matters

Neither camp has a path to a majority, so the result will likely be decided by small parties near the threshold and by coalition bargaining, with US backing now an open part of Netanyahu's pitch.

What to watch

The last polls around 23 October; whether Winter's party clears the threshold; any US or Israeli military move before 27 October.
