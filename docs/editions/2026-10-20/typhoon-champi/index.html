DevelopingTyphoon Champi

Super Typhoon Champi is now forecast to turn away from Taiwan and Luzon

Forecasters in Taiwan and the Philippines now expect no direct hit, but heavy rain in northern Taiwan on Friday and Saturday and strong winds in Luzon's far north.

The Signal · Tuesday 20 October 2026

Key facts

  • Champi, called Rosal in the Philippines, had sustained winds of 195 km/h and gusts of 240 km/h on Tuesday afternoon, PAGASA says.
  • Signal No. 1 is up for Batanes, the Babuyan Islands and parts of northern Luzon; the highest expected is No. 2 or 3.
  • Taiwan's weather agency expects the storm to turn north near Ishigaki from Thursday, sparing the island a direct hit.
  • Its effects on Taiwan are forecast to be strongest on Friday and Saturday, 23–24 October.

Where the storm is

Super Typhoon Champi entered the Philippine area of responsibility at 10:40am on Tuesday, local time, and was named Rosal. By the afternoon it was about 1,270 km east of central Luzon with sustained winds of 195 km/h and gusts of 240 km/h, moving west-northwest at 25 km/h and slightly strengthening, the weather agency PAGASA said.

PAGASA does not expect landfall. The storm's closest approach to the far north of Luzon is forecast for Thursday and Friday, passing several hundred kilometres east of Batanes. Signal No. 1 is in force for Batanes, the Babuyan Islands and parts of Cagayan, Isabela, Ilocos Norte and Catanduanes, and the highest signal expected is No. 2 or 3.

Taiwan

Taiwan's Central Weather Administration now expects Champi to turn north earlier, from Thursday near Japan's Ishigaki Island, because the Pacific high-pressure system is weakening, sparing Taiwan a direct hit, the Taipei Times reports. It still forecasts heavy to extremely heavy rain in the north and in Yilan and Hualien, with the strongest effects on Friday and Saturday. A sea warning could come late on Wednesday or on Thursday, and a land warning on Thursday night or Friday.

What comes next

PAGASA expects the storm to weaken to a typhoon by late Thursday and to leave the Philippine area by Friday evening or early Saturday. Track forecasts beyond Thursday differ between agencies, so Taiwan, Japan's southern islands and the Chinese coast are all watching the turn.

Why it matters

Until this week the storm was heading toward the northern Philippines and Taiwan. The forecast turn lowers that risk sharply, though the rain on Taiwan's mountainous north and east can still be dangerous.

What to watch

Whether the northward turn happens on Thursday as forecast; Taiwan's sea and land warnings; rainfall totals in Yilan and Hualien on 23–24 October.
