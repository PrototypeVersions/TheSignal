DevelopingUS midterms 2026

Trump's approval falls to 31% as early voting opens in record numbers

A Reuters/Ipsos poll puts Democrats 12 points ahead on the generic ballot, the widest gap of the cycle, two weeks before the 3 November midterms.

The Signal · Tuesday 20 October 2026

Key facts

  • Trump's approval is 31% in the Reuters/Ipsos poll ending Monday, the lowest of either of his terms.
  • Registered voters prefer Democrats for Congress by 47% to 35%, up from 44% to 37% two weeks earlier.
  • An ABC News/Washington Post/Ipsos poll has Democrats ahead 52% to 44% among likely voters.
  • Harris County, Texas, cast 104,201 ballots on the first day of early voting, against about 63,000 on day one in 2018.

The polls

President Trump's approval has fallen to 31% in a Reuters/Ipsos poll completed on Monday, down from 32% two weeks earlier and the lowest of either of his terms. Sixty-eight percent of respondents disapprove of his handling of the economy, including 37% of Republicans. Among registered voters, Democrats lead the generic congressional ballot by 47% to 35%, the widest margin of the campaign, and voters now rate Democrats better on the economy by 40% to 33%.

An ABC News/Washington Post/Ipsos poll released on Sunday, taken from 9 to 13 October, found the same pattern: approval at 33%, a record low in that series, and Democrats ahead by 52% to 44% among likely voters, up from three points in July. About seven in ten said gasoline is unaffordable, up from 28% in February, before the Iran war began.

Early voting

Early voting began on Monday in Texas and several other states. Harris County, which includes Houston, recorded 104,201 ballots by 6pm on the first day, far above its 2018 first-day record of about 63,000, the county clerk's figures show.

The campaign

Trump rallied on Monday near Wichita, Kansas, for Senator Roger Marshall, whose race against Democrat Adam Hamilton Cook rates a toss-up. He told the crowd to look at France to see what is at stake and asked supporters to act as if he were on the ballot, Newsweek and Fox News report. He holds rallies in Texas on Tuesday for Ken Paxton's Senate campaign. Vice President JD Vance announced $858 million in additional Hurricane Helene relief in North Carolina.

Why it matters

With fuel prices tied to the Iran war and approval at a historic low, the polls point to a sizeable swing against the president's party; record early turnout suggests the election will not be decided on enthusiasm alone.

What to watch

Further early-vote totals; whether Trump acts on fuel prices before 3 November; Obama's rally in Atlanta on 28 October; any US move on Iran before polling day.
