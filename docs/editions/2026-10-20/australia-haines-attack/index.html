DevelopingAttack on an Australian MP

Man charged over attack on MP Helen Haines, who has lost a leg

Victoria Police charged a 43-year-old local man with three offences, including harming a Commonwealth official; Haines remains in critical condition after losing a leg.

The Signal · Tuesday 20 October 2026

Key facts

  • Brendan Nugent, 43, of Tarrawingee, Victoria, is due before Wangaratta Magistrates' Court on Wednesday 21 October.
  • He faces charges of dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury and causing harm to a Commonwealth public official.
  • Haines, 65, lost a leg and doctors were fighting to save the other; she remains critical at The Alfred hospital in Melbourne.
  • Police say the attack was targeted but the motive is unclear; the national terrorism threat level stays at 'probable'.

The charges

Victoria Police on Tuesday evening, Australian time, charged Brendan Nugent, 43, of Tarrawingee near Wangaratta, over Monday's attack on the independent MP for Indi, Helen Haines. He faces three charges: dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury, and the Commonwealth offence of causing harm to a Commonwealth public official, which carries up to ten years in prison. He was held overnight and is due in Wangaratta Magistrates' Court on Wednesday, the ABC reports.

Police say a white Toyota LandCruiser hit Haines outside her electorate office on Murphy Street in Wangaratta at about 3:35pm on Monday. Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke and witnesses say she was hit twice after the vehicle made a U-turn.

Her condition and the investigation

Haines, 65, lost a leg, and doctors were fighting to save the other after surgery at The Alfred in Melbourne. She remains in critical condition, and the hospital's next update is due on Wednesday morning.

Victoria Police are leading the investigation with the joint counterterrorism team. Police allege the attack was targeted but say the motive, including whether it was political, is unclear. The Australian Federal Police commissioner, Krissy Barrett, said it is being treated as an isolated incident with no known connected threats.

The political response

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese, who had called the attack deliberate, urged Australians to turn the political temperature down. The head of ASIO, Mike Burgess, said polarisation had created a permissive environment for violence, and Opposition Leader Angus Taylor warned against crossing from robust debate into incitement. Campaigning for Victoria's 28 November state election paused on Tuesday.

Why it matters

An attack that left a sitting federal MP critically hurt has become a test of how Australia handles rising hostility toward politicians, with the security agency chief now publicly linking polarisation to violence.

What to watch

Nugent's first court appearance and any police statement on motive on Wednesday; the hospital's update on Haines; whether parliament changes security for MPs' electorate offices.
