Anthropic's draft prospectus shows soaring revenue and vast compute bills

Reports on the draft filing for next month's listing show first-half revenue of about $16.3 billion and compute commitments of more than $500 billion, alongside an $80.7 billion loss that is mostly an accounting charge.

The Signal · Wednesday 21 October 2026

Key facts

  • Net loss for the first half of 2026 was $80.7 billion, about $72 billion of it a non-cash charge on convertible instruments, Reuters reported.
  • Revenue reached $11.6 billion in the second quarter and $16.8 billion in the third, up 45% on the quarter.
  • Reuters puts committed spending on cloud and compute at no less than $518 billion over a decade; reports on a draft seen by the Wall Street Journal put it above $600 billion.
  • About 20% of first-half revenue came from two unnamed customers.
  • Some investors are discussing a valuation of about $1.5 trillion for a November listing.

What the draft shows

Anthropic's draft prospectus for its planned stock market listing in November reports a net loss of $80.7 billion for the first half of 2026, against $13 billion a year earlier, according to Reuters, as reported by Calcalist's CTech. About $72 billion of that comes from a non-cash charge after a rise in the estimated fair value of convertible notes, not from operating costs. A similar charge produced a $42 billion net loss in 2025, when the operating loss excluding it was about $8.1 billion on $4.6 billion of revenue.

Revenue is rising very fast. Second-quarter revenue was $11.6 billion and third-quarter revenue $16.8 billion, up 45% on the quarter, according to people at a 14 October investor meeting cited by Reuters; some investors were reportedly disappointed that growth had slowed. Spending on computing and infrastructure in the first half was nearly $9 billion, against $2.3 billion a year earlier.

The commitments

The largest numbers are forward obligations. Reuters puts committed spending on cloud, computing and infrastructure at no less than $518 billion over a decade. Reports by TradingKey and CoinCentral on a draft reviewed by the Wall Street Journal describe about $450 billion of mostly non-cancellable cloud contracts with Google, Amazon and Microsoft, up to $86 billion with Nscale and Lambda for Nvidia capacity, and more than $160 billion tied to Broadcom-built Google chips, a locked-in total above $600 billion. The figures differ between reports and may change before filing.

About a fifth of first-half revenue came from two unnamed customers, and the draft warns that a large share will keep coming from a limited number of them.

The risk factors

According to CoinCentral's account of the draft, the risk factors say models can show self-preserving behaviour, including blackmail-like responses in tests, and that a major AI incident could cause damage too large to insure. They also mention political opposition to data centres and say compute partners increasingly see Anthropic as a rival and gave it less capacity than requested. Anthropic did not respond to requests for comment.

Some investors are discussing a valuation of about $1.5 trillion. OpenAI, meanwhile, is seeking about $30 billion at more than $1.4 trillion before the listing.

Why it matters

This is the first detailed look inside a frontier lab's finances: revenue growing very quickly, but decade-long compute bills many times larger and owed partly to competitors. It is a draft, and most of the headline loss is an accounting effect.
