DevelopingIsrael's election

Polls tilt slightly to Eisenkot, who rules out sharing power with Netanyahu

Most new polls put Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar ahead of Likud and his camp a few seats ahead, though neither side is near a majority; Eisenkot pledged not to serve under Netanyahu in a rotation.

The Signal · Wednesday 21 October 2026

Key facts

  • Channel 12 has Yashar on 24 seats and Likud on 21, with Eisenkot's bloc at 55 against Netanyahu's 51.
  • A Walla poll gives Netanyahu's coalition 48 seats and the opposition 54; no poll gives either side 61.
  • Ofer Winter's party falls below the 3.25% threshold in three of four new polls.
  • Vice President JD Vance said he was not getting involved in Israel's election, after Trump endorsed Netanyahu.

The polls

Six days before Israel votes on 27 October, the latest polls lean slightly towards the opposition. Channel 12 gives former army chief Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party 24 seats, Likud 21 and Naftali Bennett's list 14, with Eisenkot's bloc at 55 against 51 for Netanyahu's camp and 14 for Arab parties. KAN has Yashar on 23 and Likud on 21. A Walla poll puts the governing coalition at 48 and the opposition at 54. Channel 13 shows the narrowest gap, 51 to 50. None gives either side the 61 seats needed to govern.

Smaller parties may decide the outcome. Ofer Winter's People of Israel is below the 3.25% threshold in three of the four polls compiled by Maariv and the Jerusalem Post, and KAN now gives it no seats. Arab parties, with about 14 seats, remain outside both main blocs.

No rotation

Eisenkot said on Wednesday that he would not serve under Netanyahu in any rotation agreement, and that he would resign an hour after such a deal if one were made. President Isaac Herzog is reported to favour a broad government if the vote ends in deadlock, to avoid repeated elections like those of 2019 and 2020.

In Washington, Vice President JD Vance declined to back Netanyahu, saying he was not getting involved, a contrast with Trump's endorsement on 18 October. Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth met Netanyahu at an air base in southern Israel on Monday. Netanyahu said on Tuesday that finishing the job meant bringing down Iran's government.

What comes next

The army, on its highest alert with leave frozen, says it will make sure the vote takes place without outside interference.

Why it matters

Netanyahu's camp has slipped behind in most surveys, and Eisenkot's pledge closes off the unity-rotation deal that ended earlier deadlocks, making a hung result harder to resolve.

What to watch

The last polls before polling day; whether Winter's and the Reservists' parties clear the threshold; turnout among Arab voters; any military escalation before 27 October.
