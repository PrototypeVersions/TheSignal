Edition 4, Wednesday 21 October 2026

DevelopingIran war and the Gulf

Tanker hit off Oman as Iran tells Pakistan's army chief its terms

A tanker caught fire after being struck near Hormuz on Wednesday, a day after Iran said its mines halted three ships; in Tehran, officials told Asim Munir there would be no talks without US steps first.

The Signal · Wednesday 21 October 2026

Key facts

  • UKMTO said a tanker was hit by an unknown projectile about 11 nautical miles off Bukha, Oman, on 21 October; the crew is safe.
  • Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on 20 October that mines halted three tankers; US Central Command called the claims false.
  • Brent settled at $108.79 on Tuesday and rose above $110 in Asian trading on Wednesday.
  • The toll from the Houthi strike on a Yemeni army camp in Shabwa has risen to at least 73, the provincial governor told AP.
  • Egypt's parliament votes on 26 October on sending troops to defend Saudi Arabia; approval needs 398 of 596 seats.

More attacks at sea

A tanker was struck by an unknown projectile about 11 nautical miles off Bukha, on Oman's side of the Strait of Hormuz, on Wednesday 21 October, the UK's maritime agency UKMTO said. The ship caught fire and is now at anchor, and all crew are reported safe. It was not named and no one claimed the attack.

On Tuesday Iran's Revolutionary Guards said their mines had stopped three tankers using routes Tehran calls unauthorised, two of which caught fire, and that a third, carrying more than two million barrels of crude, exploded. No ships were named. US Central Command said the claims were false and that US forces had cleared the international lanes. The data firm Kpler counts at least 18 attacks on vessels in two weeks.

Brent crude settled at $108.79 a barrel on Tuesday after touching nearly $112 during the day, then rose about 1.5% to above $110 in early Asian trading on Wednesday.

Diplomacy in Tehran

Pakistan's army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, arrived in Tehran on Tuesday on his fifth visit since the April ceasefire and met Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, President Masoud Pezeshkian and Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf. Iranian security official Mohsen Rezaei told him Iran would not send any enriched uranium abroad and would not negotiate until the US took steps to build trust; Ghalibaf listed conditions including an end to the US blockade of Iranian ports.

A Qatari outlet reported a Qatar–Pakistan proposal linking access to frozen Iranian funds to unannounced nuclear inspections, but a diplomat cited by Bloomberg disputed that a deal was close. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio accused Iran of Nazi U-boat tactics and said a deal would be hard.

The Saudi front

The death toll from Monday's Houthi missile strike on a government training camp in Shabwa, Yemen, has reached at least 73, nearly all young recruits, Governor Awad Ibn al-Wazir told AP. Saudi forces intercepted another missile and drone aimed at Riyadh on Tuesday, and Britain said RAF Typhoons will fly defensive patrols over Saudi Arabia without striking Yemen. Riyadh's main airport is operating under restrictions, and Qatar Airways has suspended flights there until 24 October.

In Cairo, some parties say they will abstain on President Sisi's request to send forces to defend Saudi Arabia, which needs a two-thirds majority on 26 October.

Why it matters

Attacks on shipping are spreading towards the Gulf of Oman while each side sets preconditions for talks, so the strait stays shut in practice and oil stays near $110. Egypt's vote would widen the Saudi–Houthi war into a regional coalition.

What to watch

Whether the tanker off Oman is identified and attributed; any US reply to Iran's conditions; Egypt's vote on 26 October; the third US carrier due by late November; Trump's pledge not to strike Iran before 3 November.
