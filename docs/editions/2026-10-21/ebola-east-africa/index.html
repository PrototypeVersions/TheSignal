Major shiftEbola outbreak in Africa

Ebola reaches Nigeria as a traveller tests positive in Abuja

Nigeria confirmed its first case of the Bundibugyo outbreak on Tuesday, a woman who reached the capital via Uganda, carrying Africa's worst Ebola epidemic into its most populous country.

The Signal · Wednesday 21 October 2026

Key facts

  • The patient fell ill on 7 October and was admitted to a private hospital in Abuja on 11 October; 44 contacts are being traced.
  • Uganda says she is Nigerian, had been on a business trip to eastern Congo and only passed through Uganda.
  • The outbreak has infected more than 9,500 people and killed more than 4,600 since mid-May, AP reports.
  • Kenya has now recorded nine infections, all linked to one patient who travelled from Congo.
  • There is no licensed vaccine or treatment for the Bundibugyo strain.

What happened

Nigeria's Health Ministry confirmed on Tuesday 20 October that a woman in Abuja has Ebola, the first case in West Africa linked to the outbreak that began in the Democratic Republic of Congo in May. She developed symptoms on 7 October and was admitted to a private hospital in the capital on 11 October, where the infection was later diagnosed. She is in isolation, and health workers are tracing 44 people she had contact with. Officials have not said when she arrived in Nigeria.

Health Minister Muhammad Pate said the government was doing everything possible to contain the case by tracing all her contacts. The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control had already stepped up surveillance two weeks earlier, after Kenya detected its first case, and a national Ebola task force has existed since June.

A dispute over where she was infected

Nigerian officials said the woman arrived from Uganda. Uganda's Ministry of Health replied that she is Nigerian, had been on a business trip to eastern Congo, and only transited through Uganda, adding that travel through the country does not show she was infected there. Uganda, which earlier reported cases and then declared itself free of the virus, insists it has no confirmed active cases.

The wider outbreak

The Bundibugyo-strain epidemic has infected more than 9,500 people and killed more than 4,600 since mid-May, according to AP. The World Health Organization calls it the fastest-spreading Ebola outbreak on record and says it is on course to exceed the 2014–2016 West African epidemic, which killed about 11,000 people. Africa CDC's director general, Jean Kaseya, called the threat extremely serious, with nearly half of cases dying.

Inside Congo the virus has reached an eighth province, Nord-Ubangi near the Central African Republic border, while Ituri still accounts for nearly three-quarters of confirmed cases. The WHO's Africa director, Mohamed Yakub Janabi, said pockets of infection were generally falling but the outbreak was spreading geographically. Kenya has recorded nine infections, all traced to a man who travelled by road from Congo to Kampala and flew to Nairobi on 3 October.

There is no licensed vaccine or treatment for the Bundibugyo strain, which leaves isolation and contact tracing as the main tools.

What comes next

Nigeria will not know for some weeks whether the case has spread. It contained its previous Ebola outbreak at 20 cases and declared it over within three months; Abuja and the private hospital where the patient was first treated are now the focus.

Why it matters

A case carried by air to Africa's most populous country shows the outbreak can now jump far beyond Congo's neighbours. How well Nigeria traces 44 contacts will test whether the epidemic stays a regional crisis.

What to watch

Test results among the 44 contacts over the next three weeks; whether the hospital in Abuja reports infected staff; any new WHO risk assessment or emergency committee; further cases in Kenya.
