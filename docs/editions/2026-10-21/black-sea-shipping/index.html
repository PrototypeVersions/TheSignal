NewDrone attacks on Black Sea shipping

NATO sends more planes and radars to the Black Sea after drone attacks on ships

After six suspected Russian drone attacks on merchant ships in ten days off Romania and Bulgaria, NATO said on Tuesday it is deploying patrol aircraft, radars, drones and intelligence staff.

The Signal · Wednesday 21 October 2026

Key facts

  • NATO's military command said on 20 October it is sending maritime patrol aircraft, radars, drones, explosives teams and intelligence staff.
  • Kaja Kallas counted six suspected Russian drone attacks in 10 days; at least 23 sailors are missing or dead.
  • Romanian F-16s shot down two Geran-type drones over Romanian waters on Saturday 17 October.
  • Bulgaria says a US P-8 Poseidon has begun reconnaissance flights over the sea at its request.
  • Moscow denies involvement; NATO, Romania and Bulgaria have not formally assigned blame.

What NATO decided

NATO will send more maritime patrol aircraft, radar systems, drones, explosive ordnance teams and intelligence personnel to the Black Sea, Colonel Martin O'Donnell of the alliance's military headquarters said on Tuesday 20 October, after ambassadors of the 32 members met in Brussels. He said the priority was a clearer picture of what is happening at sea, through more sensors and shared data. Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev said a US P-8 Poseidon had already begun reconnaissance patrols after Sofia asked for help.

The attacks

Civilian ships have been hit repeatedly off the coasts of Romania and Bulgaria, both NATO and EU members. On 5 October a ship caught fire and sank in Romanian waters, killing two people. Around 13 October drones struck two cargo ships off Bulgaria; one sank and 11 sailors were reported missing. Last week two chemical tankers were set ablaze near Romania's coast, with seven sailors missing, and at least five other cargo ships have been hit in the area. In all, at least 23 sailors are missing or dead, according to Al Jazeera's count.

On Saturday evening two Romanian F-16s shot down two Geran attack drones, a type associated with Russia's arsenal, over Romanian territorial waters. Romania has recorded 35 drone incursions this year, against nine in 2025.

Who is blamed

NATO, Romania and Bulgaria have not formally blamed any country, and Russia denies involvement. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas called the strikes suspected Russian attacks and a dangerous escalation, and said the Kremlin was probably trying to cut Ukraine's grain export earnings. Attacks on ships in international waters, outside allied territory, are a hard case for the alliance's defence commitments.

Why it matters

Sinking merchant ships next to NATO coastlines is a new kind of pressure on the alliance and on Ukraine's main export route, and NATO has now chosen to answer with surveillance rather than escorts.

What to watch

Whether investigators attribute the attacks; any decision on naval escorts; further hits on ships bound for Ukrainian ports; grain freight and insurance costs.
