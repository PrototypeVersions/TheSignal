Edition 4, Wednesday 21 October 2026

Major shiftRussia–Ukraine war

Witkoff meets Putin after Oslo talks, and Russia frees an American

Trump's envoy took a peace framework from Tuesday's talks with Zelensky and European leaders in Oslo to Putin in St Petersburg on Wednesday, where Russia released US prisoner Stephen Hubbard.

The Signal · Wednesday 21 October 2026

Key facts

  • Witkoff met Zelensky in Oslo on 20 October with the leaders of Norway and Finland and officials from France, the UK, Germany, Poland, the EU and NATO.
  • A US official told Axios there was meaningful progress; Zelensky wants a three-way meeting with Trump and Putin, which Moscow has rejected.
  • Putin met Witkoff at the Constantine Palace on 21 October and announced the release of Stephen Hubbard, a retired teacher from Michigan.
  • Russia launched 186 drones and cruise missiles overnight; four people were killed, two of them in Kyiv.
  • EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said US negotiators had been pushing Ukraine towards surrender for almost two years.

From Oslo to St Petersburg

US envoy Steve Witkoff met President Volodymyr Zelensky in Oslo on Tuesday 20 October, joined by Norway's prime minister, Jonas Gahr Støre, Finland's president, Alexander Stubb, and representatives of France, the UK, Germany, Poland, the EU and NATO. Jared Kushner took part by video. The aim, according to Axios, was a common framework proposal to put to Russia. A US official said Ukraine had brought new ideas and that there was strong alignment at working level; Stubb spoke of real progress without giving details.

Topics included security guarantees and a ceasefire on energy infrastructure. Kushner pressed Kyiv to stop striking Russian refineries, a source told Axios; Zelensky said he would stop if Moscow did the same. Zelensky said the next step should be a meeting with Putin and Trump, a format Russia has so far turned down.

Witkoff flew on to St Petersburg and met Putin at the Constantine Palace on Wednesday morning. Putin announced during the meeting that Stephen Hubbard, a retired American teacher from Michigan sentenced in 2024 to nearly seven years for allegedly fighting for Ukraine, had been freed and taken to the airport. Witkoff told Putin that because of him Hubbard was going home. No outcome on the peace proposal had been announced by Wednesday morning. Reports say Putin would rather meet Trump in China next month without Ukraine present.

Pressure on Kyiv, pushback from Europe

Trump said on Tuesday that Zelensky has to want to settle the war, that Russia keeps grinding forward and that things will get worse without a deal. Zelensky told US senators that arms deliveries had been delayed, including missiles allies paid for through NATO's PURL scheme, though he said intelligence sharing was now working again.

In the European Parliament, Kaja Kallas said American negotiators had been trying to push Ukraine into surrender for almost two years and that Russia was the only party that did not want peace.

On the ground

Overnight into Wednesday Russia launched 186 drones, 88 of them jet-powered, plus cruise missiles, Ukraine's air force said; 150 were downed or jammed. Two people were killed when a drone hit the 20th floor of a tower block in Kyiv, two elderly residents died near Kharkiv, and a university building in Kremenchuk caught fire.

Why it matters

For the first time in weeks, Washington, Kyiv and the main European capitals put a shared proposal to Moscow, and the Kremlin answered with a goodwill gesture. Whether Putin moves on substance, or only on prisoners, decides if this round differs from earlier ones.

What to watch

Any readout from the Kremlin or White House on the framework; whether Russia agrees to three-way talks; the energy ceasefire; Patriot and PURL deliveries; whether a Trump–Putin meeting in China is fixed for November.
