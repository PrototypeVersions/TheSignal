DevelopingFrance unrest

France extends curfews and counts 3,000 damaged shops before Saturday's protests

The government said curfews will be set wherever needed and promised a massive police deployment for Saturday's marches, as estimates of riot damage doubled.

The Signal · Wednesday 21 October 2026

Key facts

  • At least 3,000 shops were damaged, more than in the 2023 riots; Carrefour says 50 of its stores were hit and 20 looted.
  • Paris imposed a night curfew around Place de la République and Les Halles on Tuesday and banned carrying petrol in cans.
  • Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez says 2,513 people were taken into custody; nearly 150 have been jailed, according to the justice minister.
  • Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu meets unions on Wednesday and political parties on Thursday; new protests are called for 24 October.

Calm, under curfew

France had a second quieter night as targeted curfews stayed in force. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said they had helped bring calm and that prefects and mayors would set new ones wherever the situation required. In Paris, police imposed a curfew from late evening around Place de la République and Les Halles, banned gatherings on the Champs-Élysées and near stations and public buildings, and temporarily barred people from carrying petrol or gas in cans or bottles.

Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez says 2,513 people have been taken into custody since the unrest began. Justice Minister Gérald Darmanin says nearly 150 have been jailed and more than 800 brought before the courts. A man believed to be linked to the far left was arrested over the fire that destroyed the historic town hall of Limoges, a case now with counter-terrorism prosecutors.

The cost

At least 3,000 shops were damaged, twice the estimate of a day earlier and more than the roughly 2,500 businesses hit in the 2023 riots, Euronews reports. Carrefour said 50 of its stores were damaged and 20 looted, with losses its chief executive put at several hundred million euros. On Monday the CAC 40 fell 1.1% and France's ten-year borrowing cost rose to about 4.94%. Firms affected can use state-funded short-time working, and the digital minister met online platforms about looted goods being resold.

What comes next

Lecornu meets five unions on Wednesday to discuss protecting the right to protest, and party leaders on Thursday; high-school students were not invited. He has promised a massive security deployment for Saturday 24 October, when Yellow Vest groups and students plan marches without the main unions, who have called their own protest for 5 November. Parliament agreed to open an inquiry into intelligence and policing failures. President Macron, on a Gulf tour that took him to Qatar, rejected Nuñez's offer to resign. Unrest has also spread to Italy, where Milan's luxury shops boarded up on Monday.

Why it matters

The curfews have stopped the nightly rioting for now, but the movement's grievances over schools and living costs are unresolved and Saturday is its first big test under the new restrictions.

What to watch

Whether nights stay calm; the outcome of Wednesday's union talks; turnout and policing on Saturday 24 October; whether a state of emergency is declared; Macron's return.
