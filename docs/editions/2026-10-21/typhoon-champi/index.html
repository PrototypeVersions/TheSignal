DevelopingTyphoon Champi

Champi regains top strength and heads for Japan's Sakishima Islands

The super typhoon re-intensified to a central pressure near 900 hPa, and Japan's weather agency now expects it to pass near Ishigaki and Miyako on Friday evening before turning towards Kyushu.

The Signal · Wednesday 21 October 2026

Key facts

  • Japan's weather agency put Champi's central pressure at 900 hPa on the morning of 21 October, with gusts of about 165 knots.
  • It is forecast to pass near the Sakishima Islands, including Ishigaki, Miyako and Yonaguni, on the evening of Friday 23 October.
  • Many models then turn it north-east over the East China Sea, with a possible landfall in Kyushu.
  • Taiwan's weather bureau expects the storm's closest approach between Friday and Saturday morning, with heavy rain in the north and east.

Stronger again

Super Typhoon Champi, the 30th named storm of the Pacific season, strengthened again on Tuesday evening after weakening near Guam. On Wednesday morning Japan's Meteorological Agency described it as large and violent, with a central pressure of 900 hPa, sustained winds of 115 knots and gusts of 165 knots, moving west-north-west at about 13 knots across the Philippine Sea. Estimates based on US military forecasts put its peak one-minute winds on Tuesday at about 295 km/h, equivalent to a category 5 hurricane.

The track

Forecasters now expect Champi to curve gradually north-west and pass near Japan's Sakishima Islands, the chain that includes Ishigaki, Miyako and Yonaguni, on the evening and night of Friday 23 October while still very strong. The agency warned of a high chance of heavy rain, storm-force winds and high waves across Okinawa, especially Sakishima. After that, many models turn the storm north and north-east over the East China Sea, weakening, with a possible landfall in Kyushu; Japan's forecast cone now reaches into western Japan for early next week. Weathernews notes a wide spread between agencies.

That is a shift from Monday's forecasts, which had the turn starting near Ishigaki on Thursday. Taiwan's Central Weather Administration says the track has moved farther west, and the island is not yet in the clear.

Taiwan

The administration said a sea warning could be issued and a land warning was possible from Wednesday night, covering northern Taiwan, Yilan and Hualien, depending on the track. Whatever path the storm takes, its closest approach is expected from Friday to Saturday morning. The north-east monsoon is already bringing rain to Yilan and the mountains around Taipei, and heavy to extremely heavy rain is possible in the north and east on Friday. Keelung has a 72% chance of gale-force winds within five days, according to the bureau's probabilities.

Why it matters

A storm near the strongest possible intensity is now aimed at inhabited Japanese islands rather than open sea, and its later turn keeps both Taiwan and Kyushu in play.

What to watch

Japan's warnings for Okinawa and Sakishima on Thursday; Taiwan's land warning; the timing of the turn north; whether Kyushu enters the track early next week.
