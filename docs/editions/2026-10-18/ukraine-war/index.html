Major shiftRussia–Ukraine war

Ukraine sends 1,000 drones into Russia as Washington waives sanctions

Russia says it downed 1,015 drones over 15 regions, and St. Petersburg's oil terminal caught fire. Two days earlier, Trump waived nearly all of the new US penalties on Russian energy.

The Signal · Sunday 18 October 2026 · Updated 5:58pm CT

Fires broke out at the Petersburg Oil Terminal and the city's Ruchi depot; a logistics hub for the retailer Ozon and a VK data center were also targeted, both companies said. St. Petersburg's governor reported one dead and five wounded. Russian strikes on Zaporizhzhia killed a woman, and Zelenskyy said attacks on energy sites in four regions left tens of thousands of families without power.

The 'energy ceasefire' Trump announced on 11 October has not held. Kyiv counts 76 Russian attacks on its energy, oil and gas sectors since then, and Ukraine resumed strikes on Russian oil on Thursday. Late on Friday, two days before they were due to take effect, Trump signed an order waiving almost all of the Graham Act's measures, including tariffs of up to 100% on major buyers of Russian oil. Zelenskyy called it a gift to Putin, and lawmakers from both parties objected.

Why it matters

Washington's main lever on Moscow's oil revenue has been set aside with no end date, while both sides escalate against each other's energy systems going into winter. Diplomacy is weaker, and the energy war stronger, than a week ago.

What to watch

Whether Congress moves to block the waiver or the separate Russian diesel deal. Russia's response to the St. Petersburg strikes.

Since this morning

  • Update, 5:58pm CT

    Zelenskyy said Kyiv is done with the energy ceasefire Trump announced, accusing Russia of trying to cause a blackout. Russian strikes across Ukraine killed at least 10 civilians and injured 73 over the past day, regional authorities said, most of the dead in Donetsk region. Secretary of State Rubio described the sanctions waiver as a 60-day pause to study its economic effects.
