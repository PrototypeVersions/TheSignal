How it works

The Signal is a daily briefing that asks one question: what changed in the world today that changes our picture of it?

One edition a day

There is no feed and no breaking-news banner. Each morning we publish around ten world stories and a short Frontiers section on AI and science, and each evening we add what has changed since. Every story fits in a few lines on the front page; its full report, with key facts, background, what to watch and every source, is one click away.

Every region, every day

The region map covers North America, Latin America, Europe, Africa, the Middle East, Asia and Oceania. Each region shows its stories from the edition plus news that did not make the main page. If nothing important happened somewhere, we say so.

Stories belong to threads

A thread is a situation we keep following: an election, a war, an outbreak. Each day's story is the latest entry in its thread, so you can see how a situation got here. Thread pages keep the whole timeline.

Change and momentum

Every story carries two labels. Change says how much today's news altered the picture:

Major shiftOur understanding of the situation changed substantially today.
NewA situation that matters and that we were not following yesterday.
DevelopingReal movement, but in the direction we already expected.
QuietStill important, little changed. Usually left out of the edition.

Momentum says which way the situation is moving: Intensifying, Holding or Easing. We use words rather than scores on purpose; a score like 7.8 implies a precision nobody has.

Sources and corrections

Every story links the reporting it is based on, and we paraphrase rather than reprint. When we get something wrong, the correction is added to the edition it fixes.
