How it works

The Signal is a once-a-day briefing that asks one question: what changed in the world today that changes our picture of it?

One edition a day, and then it stops

There is no feed and no breaking-news banner. Each morning we publish around ten world stories, a short Frontiers section on AI and science and a pulse check on every region. If nothing important happened somewhere, we say so.

Stories belong to threads

A thread is a situation we keep following: an election, a war, an outbreak. Each day's story is the latest entry in its thread, so you can see how a situation got here. Thread pages keep the whole timeline.

Change and momentum

Every story carries two labels. Change says how much today's news altered the picture:

Major shiftOur understanding of the situation changed substantially today.
NewA situation that matters and that we were not following yesterday.
DevelopingReal movement, but in the direction we already expected.
QuietStill important, little changed. Usually left out of the edition.

Momentum says which way the situation is moving: Intensifying, Holding or Easing. We use words rather than scores on purpose; a score like 7.8 implies a precision nobody has.

Forecasts

We keep a small set of questions with exact resolution rules and give our odds. When evidence moves, the odds move, with the reason logged. Past numbers are never edited, and when a question resolves it is scored, so you can judge whether we are any good.
