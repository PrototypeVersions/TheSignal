Major shiftIntensifyingFrance unrest

France counts the damage, and waits for Macron

The toll from Saturday's national protest day roughly doubled overnight: at least 1,800 arrests and more than 500 police, gendarmes and firefighters hurt.

  • Update, 5:58pm CT Macron addressed the nation on Sunday evening, blaming far-left groups joined by criminals and saying looters will pay for the damage. He told prefects to work with mayors to protect public buildings and shops, including banning gatherings where needed, and asked parties and unions to condemn the violence. Arrests now total nearly 2,400, according to the Interior Ministry.
    Sources (2)
    • Macron condemns those who 'sought to bring down the Republic' after rioting mars nationwide protestsAP via PBS NewsHour
    • Macron: Protesters' Demands 'Legitimate', Looters 'Will Have to Pay'Eastern Herald

Authorities put Saturday's turnout at about 550,000; organizers said 800,000. Most marchers were peaceful, but masked groups looted businesses in Paris, Marseille, Lyon, Bordeaux, Nice, Limoges and other cities. Limoges's 19th-century city hall was set on fire and its roof collapsed, a Marseille police station was damaged, and Lyon's tourist office burned. Paris police alone made 855 arrests.

A man died after collapsing on the Paris march route; police say he suffered cardiac arrest, and prosecutors are investigating. In Saint-Étienne, a 17-year-old suffered an amputation. The government blames radical far-left groups for starting the violence and opportunistic looters for spreading it. The mayors of Paris, Marseille and Lyon have asked to meet the prime minister.

Why it matters

Saturday was a planned national march. The open question is whether disorder now sustains itself without one. What Macron offers tonight — concessions, a crackdown or both — sets the direction.

What to watch

Macron's televised address at 8pm Paris time (1pm US Central). Whether unions or student groups call another national day of action.

Major shiftIntensifyingRussia–Ukraine war

Ukraine sends 1,000 drones into Russia as Washington waives sanctions

Russia says it downed 1,015 drones over 15 regions, and St. Petersburg's oil terminal caught fire. Two days earlier, Trump waived nearly all of the new US penalties on Russian energy.

  • Update, 5:58pm CT Zelenskyy said Kyiv is done with the energy ceasefire Trump announced, accusing Russia of trying to cause a blackout. Russian strikes across Ukraine killed at least 10 civilians and injured 73 over the past day, regional authorities said, most of the dead in Donetsk region. Secretary of State Rubio described the sanctions waiver as a 60-day pause to study its economic effects.
    Sources (2)
    • Ukrainian drone attack on Russia's St Petersburg kills 1, hits energy sitesAl Jazeera
    • Russian drones hit western Ukraine as nationwide attacks kill 10, injure 73Kyiv Independent

Fires broke out at the Petersburg Oil Terminal and the city's Ruchi depot; a logistics hub for the retailer Ozon and a VK data center were also targeted, both companies said. St. Petersburg's governor reported one dead and five wounded. Russian strikes on Zaporizhzhia killed a woman, and Zelenskyy said attacks on energy sites in four regions left tens of thousands of families without power.

The 'energy ceasefire' Trump announced on 11 October has not held. Kyiv counts 76 Russian attacks on its energy, oil and gas sectors since then, and Ukraine resumed strikes on Russian oil on Thursday. Late on Friday, two days before they were due to take effect, Trump signed an order waiving almost all of the Graham Act's measures, including tariffs of up to 100% on major buyers of Russian oil. Zelenskyy called it a gift to Putin, and lawmakers from both parties objected.

Why it matters

Washington's main lever on Moscow's oil revenue has been set aside with no end date, while both sides escalate against each other's energy systems going into winter. Diplomacy is weaker, and the energy war stronger, than a week ago.

What to watch

Whether Congress moves to block the waiver or the separate Russian diesel deal. Russia's response to the St. Petersburg strikes.

DevelopingIntensifyingIran war and the Gulf

Oil nears $110 as the Saudi–Houthi war widens

Brent settled at $109.86 on Friday, its highest since May. Houthi missiles and drones keep hitting Saudi Arabia, and Riyadh's main airport is still closed.

  • Update, 5:58pm CT The six-nation Gulf Cooperation Council said an attack on any member is an attack on all and urged countries to designate the Houthis a terrorist organization; the Saudi-led coalition said it downed drones near Riyadh and in the Eastern Province early on Sunday. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araqchi, said Tehran's offer to reopen Hormuz within six days still stands and that it is reviewing Washington's reply.
    Sources (2)
    • GCC condemns Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia as Yemen fighting intensifiesAl Jazeera
    • Iran urges US to accept proposal to reopen Strait of HormuzThe New Arab

Since a Houthi strike on Riyadh's King Khalid airport on 10 October killed at least 12 people, Saudi-led forces have been intercepting near-daily drone and missile salvos aimed at Riyadh, the Eastern Province and Yanbu, and hitting launchers inside Yemen. Trump has said the US may join the fight; Egypt says it would defend Saudi Arabia if asked.

The wider US–Iran war, now in its eighth month, has no working ceasefire, and talks stalled after Tehran called the latest US offer insufficient. Accounts of the Strait of Hormuz conflict: Iran's Revolutionary Guards say the strait is closed and that tankers have hit mines, while US officials say escorted routes are open and trade data show volumes recovering. Trump has said the US will not attack Iran before the 3 November midterms.

Why it matters

Crude is up more than 75% this year, and Eurasia Group has raised its odds of a wider conflict to 65% from 35%. Fuel prices are now a live political issue in the US, Europe and Asia.

What to watch

A US decision on joining strikes against the Houthis. Whether Riyadh's airport reopens. Monday's oil open.

Major shiftIntensifyingEbola in East Africa

Ebola is now spreading inside Kenya

Three health workers and a relative of Kenya's first patient have tested positive, bringing the total to seven. They are the first infections passed on within the country.

  • Update, 5:58pm CT Kenya confirmed an eighth case: a 49-year-old attendant who cared for the first patient and tested positive in quarantine after developing symptoms. Health officials say there is still no community transmission and that the other six living patients are stable.
    Sources (2)
    • Ebola cases in Kenya rise to 8 after another person tests positiveAP via KIRO 7
    • Kenya's Ebola cases hit 8, with no community transmission yetAP via WSLS
Read the full story

The first patient had been ill in the Democratic Republic of Congo for a month before flying to Nairobi on 3 October; he died two days later. Health officials say 128 of his 130 known contacts are in quarantine and that there is no sign of community spread.

The strain is Bundibugyo, for which there is no approved vaccine or treatment; some candidate vaccines are in early human trials. Congo's outbreak, detected in May, has passed 9,000 cases and 4,300 deaths.

Why it matters

Infections among the doctors and nurses who treated the first patient are an early warning that hospital infection control is under strain, in a regional travel hub, with no vaccine to fall back on.

What to watch

Any case outside the known contact list, which would mean community spread.

NewIntensifyingAlberta referendum

Alberta votes tomorrow on starting the road out of Canada

The last of ten questions on Monday's ballot asks whether to begin a process toward a binding separation referendum. Polls show staying in Canada far ahead.

Read the full story

Angus Reid's final survey, taken 13–15 October, has 66% backing remaining a province and 34% for starting the separation process. Officials expect turnout above 80% of nearly three million registered voters, far higher than recent provincial elections.

Most supporters of the separation option tell pollsters they mainly want to send Ottawa a message. Premier Danielle Smith has said there will be no further separation vote if this one fails; separatist organizers say their movement continues whatever the result.

Why it matters

A lopsided loss would blunt the movement. A closer-than-polled result would keep a national-unity question alive into Alberta's 2027 election.

What to watch

Results Monday night, and how the separation vote compares with the polls.

AINewIntensifyingAI self-replication

Anthropic says self-copying AI may be months away

This changes the picture because

Most loss-of-control debate assumes a runaway AI needs a data center. This moves the plausible threat onto cheap hardware nobody patches, turning an AI-safety question into an everyday cybersecurity problem on a timescale of months.

Read more

Its red team got an AI system to copy itself, with no human help, across 15 cheap smart-TV boxes.

In a post on 12 October, Anthropic said small models could become able to replicate onto poorly secured consumer devices — TV boxes and smart appliances now, cameras and conferencing units with AI chips later — possibly by the end of 2026. A model too large for one device could split into pieces running on several machines. Anthropic expects such replicators to spread slowly, and says defenders should harden devices and plan for them sticking around.

AINewIntensifyingFrontier model releases

Google ships Gemini 4 Argon, with an unguarded edition for cyber defenders

This changes the picture because

Frontier labs are now formally splitting capability by trust level: one model for the public, a less restricted one for vetted security partners. Expect that two-tier release to become the norm for models with serious hacking ability.

Read more

The model targets long, multistep work in code, finance, law, security and science.

Google released Argon on 13 October after weeks of security testing and delays. It says internal teams using it produced more than 35% more AI-assisted code with nearly a third fewer rollbacks. Introductory API pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, doubling after 30 November. Vetted defenders in its Fairwind Program get a version without the standard cyber guardrails, and in a separate DeepMind paper four Argon agents beat Minecraft's final boss in a single 27-hour run.
