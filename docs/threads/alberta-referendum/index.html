Alberta referendum

Albertans vote on 19 October on ten questions, the last asking whether to begin a process toward a binding referendum on leaving Canada. Polls show staying far ahead, and more than 43% of voters cast ballots early.

DevelopingHoldingas of 19 Oct 2026

  1. Monday 19 October 2026
    DevelopingHolding

    Alberta votes on separation after 1.3 million ballots cast early

    Elections Alberta says 43.1% of eligible voters had already voted before Monday. Polls close at 8pm Mountain time (9pm Central).

    The final Angus Reid poll had 66% planning to vote to stay a province. Among those who would back a binding separation vote, 60% told the pollster they mainly wanted to send Ottawa a message and 37% said they want an independent country. Premier Danielle Smith has said she would seek a binding referendum if the separation option wins a majority; Prime Minister Mark Carney campaigned for staying in Canada.

    Why it matters. Heavy early voting points to the high turnout officials expected. A clear loss would blunt the movement; a closer-than-polled result would keep it alive into Alberta's 2027 election.

  2. Sunday 18 October 2026
    NewIntensifying

    Alberta votes tomorrow on starting the road out of Canada

    The last of ten questions on Monday's ballot asks whether to begin a process toward a binding separation referendum. Polls show staying in Canada far ahead.

    Angus Reid's final survey, taken 13–15 October, has 66% backing remaining a province and 34% for starting the separation process. Officials expect turnout above 80% of nearly three million registered voters, far higher than recent provincial elections.

    Most supporters of the separation option tell pollsters they mainly want to send Ottawa a message. Premier Danielle Smith has said there will be no further separation vote if this one fails; separatist organizers say their movement continues whatever the result.

    Why it matters. A lopsided loss would blunt the movement. A closer-than-polled result would keep a national-unity question alive into Alberta's 2027 election.
