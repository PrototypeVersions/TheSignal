AI and work

Measured evidence of AI changing hiring, jobs and the shape of work, and early attempts by AI companies to share gains with people displaced.

DevelopingIntensifyingas of 19 Oct 2026

Following since 17 October 2026. 2 updates in The Signal.

  1. Monday 19 October 2026
    DevelopingIntensifying

    Anthropic sets out a fund that could pay people displaced by AI

    The 5% share pledge would sit in a company-owned LLC, needs stockholder approval, and could make direct payments to groups hit by job disruption.

    The board could add 2% more each time the company's value rises 50% above an undisclosed baseline. The fund may give large grants or make direct payments to specific groups, with labor-market disruption named as the example, but not fund politics or lobbying. With earlier cofounder and employee pledges, about 20% of shares are now earmarked for public benefit, Forbes reports. Critics include David Sacks, who co-chairs the president's science council and questioned whether such a vehicle serves the public; governance details are due in the coming months.

    This changes the picture because An AI lab is pre-committing part of its own upside to the people its products may put out of work. Whether it pays out at scale will be an early test of who captures AI's gains.

    At one AI lab, junior hiring has collapsed

    Anthropic says junior roles fell from 27% to 9% of its technical hires.

    • Update, 5:58pm CT Anthropic said on Sunday it will put 5% of its shares into a public benefit fund, adding 2% for every 50% rise in its value, citing the pace of AI progress. Ideas include large nonprofit grants or direct payments to specific groups; governance details are due in the coming months.

    The company's own report adds that by August, AI agents directed by individual employees were logging six times as many working hours as the people directing them.

    This changes the picture because It is one company, and an AI lab at that. But it is an early, measured case of the pattern economists worry about most: AI absorbing entry-level work first, and with it the usual path into a profession.
