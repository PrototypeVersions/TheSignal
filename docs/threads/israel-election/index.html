Israel's election

Israel votes on 27 October in wartime. Polls show neither Netanyahu's bloc nor the opposition near a 61-seat majority, and Trump has endorsed Netanyahu.

    NewIntensifying

    Israel's election is deadlocked, and Trump endorses Netanyahu

    Four polls published Sunday leave neither bloc near the 61 seats needed to govern, eight days before the 27 October vote.

    The polls, compiled by the Jerusalem Post, give Netanyahu's bloc between 49 and 53 seats and the opposition bloc between 50 and 54. Former army chief Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party averages 21.5 seats against 19 for Likud. Trump has endorsed Netanyahu, who has set out a doctrine favoring pre-emptive strikes and buffer zones.

    The campaign runs alongside the West Bank crisis: there were still no arrests on Sunday over the settler attacks on European diplomats in Taybeh and on a Washington Post photographer, and Netanyahu said there was no compelling evidence that settlers attacked the diplomats. US officials visited Taybeh on Sunday.

    Why it matters. The next government will shape Israel's part in the regional war and its West Bank policy. Polls point to a hung Knesset and weeks of coalition bargaining in wartime.
