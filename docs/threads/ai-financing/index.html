Paying for AI

The compute build-out is increasingly financed with expensive debt, and markets are pushing back: chip stocks have had their longest losing streak since 2008.

DevelopingIntensifyingas of 19 Oct 2026

Following since 17 October 2026. 2 updates in The Signal.

  1. Monday 19 October 2026
    DevelopingIntensifying

    Chip stocks suffer their longest slide since 2008 as AI debt gets dearer

    The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 11.4% last week, and the cloud provider CoreWeave dropped 12% on Friday alone.

    New details of Anthropic's chip financing, first reported by the Financial Times, show the cost: a $60 billion package for about 1.5 gigawatts of Google TPUs, with roughly $42 billion of senior debt guaranteed by Broadcom and $18 billion of junior debt. Blackstone is expected to take $9 billion of the junior slice at a 10% coupon, while similarly rated borrowers pay around 7%; KBRA rates Anthropic one notch below investment grade. Separately, Microsoft has walked away as anchor tenant of Nscale's planned UK site, which may not get grid power until the 2030s.

    This changes the picture because Investors are starting to charge for the risk in the AI build-out rather than just fund it. If financing costs keep rising while chip stocks fall, the pace of new data centers will be set by credit markets as much as by demand.

    The AI build-out is running on more expensive debt

    Anthropic has offered a 10% coupon on $9 billion of junior debt, and OpenAI's ad business looks set to miss its target.

    • Update, 5:58pm CT Microsoft has pulled out as anchor tenant of Nscale's planned data center in Loughton, England, the Telegraph reports; the site may not get a grid connection until the 2030s. Nscale, which is preparing a stock listing and says Microsoft and Anthropic make up about 85% of its contracts, says the relationship remains strong.
      Sources (1)
      • Microsoft pulls out of UK data centre deal with Nscale, Telegraph reportsReuters via Investing.com

    According to the Financial Times, Anthropic offered Blackstone 10% on $9 billion of junior debt within a $60 billion financing for Google-designed chips; four months ago it borrowed about $4.4 billion at 8.5%. The Information reports that OpenAI told investors to expect $2.4 billion in advertising revenue this year and is likely to fall short.

    This changes the picture because Frontier compute is now financed like power plants, at rising rates each round. That ties the pace of AI progress to credit markets and to revenue, not only to research.
