Russia–Ukraine war

The full-scale war is in its fifth year. Both sides are trading mass strikes on each other's energy systems as winter nears, a US-announced energy ceasefire has collapsed, and Washington has paused new sanctions on Russia for up to 60 days.

DevelopingIntensifyingas of 19 Oct 2026

24 February 2022. 2 updates in The Signal.

  1. Monday 19 October 2026
    DevelopingIntensifying

    Russia strikes back at Ukraine's grid, cutting power to 550,000 homes

    A day after Ukraine's 1,000-drone raid on Russia, Moscow fired about 270 drones and a barrage of missiles overnight, hitting energy sites in 11 regions, Zelenskyy says.

    Ukraine's air force counted 269 drones, 59 of them jet-powered, plus Iskander ballistic and cruise missiles; it says it downed 243 drones and four missiles, with hits at 25 locations. Zelenskyy said 16 ballistic missiles were aimed mainly at the Kyiv region and that more than ten energy facilities were damaged. In Kyiv one person was killed and eight injured, according to AP; across Ukraine, Russian attacks over the past day killed three and injured 53, the Kyiv Independent reports. Russia's Defence Ministry says it hit a logistics hub, a data center and a cargo ship off Odesa.

    Zelenskyy told NBC that US intelligence sharing, blocked for a period, has resumed. Rubio says Trump will take up to 60 days to study the sanctions he waived on Friday, and Congress is in recess until after the 3 November midterms, so no quick move to override him is possible.

    Why it matters. Both sides are now hitting each other's energy systems at a scale of hundreds of drones a night as the heating season begins, while the main US pressure on Moscow is paused for up to two months. Nothing on the table is slowing the energy war.

  2. Sunday 18 October 2026
    Major shiftIntensifying

    Ukraine sends 1,000 drones into Russia as Washington waives sanctions

    Russia says it downed 1,015 drones over 15 regions, and St. Petersburg's oil terminal caught fire. Two days earlier, Trump waived nearly all of the new US penalties on Russian energy.

    • Update, 5:58pm CT Zelenskyy said Kyiv is done with the energy ceasefire Trump announced, accusing Russia of trying to cause a blackout. Russian strikes across Ukraine killed at least 10 civilians and injured 73 over the past day, regional authorities said, most of the dead in Donetsk region. Secretary of State Rubio described the sanctions waiver as a 60-day pause to study its economic effects.
      Sources (2)
      • Ukrainian drone attack on Russia's St Petersburg kills 1, hits energy sitesAl Jazeera
      • Russian drones hit western Ukraine as nationwide attacks kill 10, injure 73Kyiv Independent

    Fires broke out at the Petersburg Oil Terminal and the city's Ruchi depot; a logistics hub for the retailer Ozon and a VK data center were also targeted, both companies said. St. Petersburg's governor reported one dead and five wounded. Russian strikes on Zaporizhzhia killed a woman, and Zelenskyy said attacks on energy sites in four regions left tens of thousands of families without power.

    The 'energy ceasefire' Trump announced on 11 October has not held. Kyiv counts 76 Russian attacks on its energy, oil and gas sectors since then, and Ukraine resumed strikes on Russian oil on Thursday. Late on Friday, two days before they were due to take effect, Trump signed an order waiving almost all of the Graham Act's measures, including tariffs of up to 100% on major buyers of Russian oil. Zelenskyy called it a gift to Putin, and lawmakers from both parties objected.

    Why it matters. Washington's main lever on Moscow's oil revenue has been set aside with no end date, while both sides escalate against each other's energy systems going into winter. Diplomacy is weaker, and the energy war stronger, than a week ago.
