Russia–Ukraine war

The full-scale war is in its fifth year. Both sides are escalating long-range strikes on energy infrastructure while a US-announced energy ceasefire has failed to hold.

  1. Sunday 18 October 2026
    Major shiftIntensifying

    Ukraine sends 1,000 drones into Russia as Washington waives sanctions

    Russia says it downed 1,015 drones over 15 regions, and St. Petersburg's oil terminal caught fire. Hours earlier, Trump waived nearly all of the new US penalties on Russian energy.

    Fires broke out at the Petersburg Oil Terminal and the city's Ruchi depot; a logistics hub for the retailer Ozon and a VK data center were also hit. St. Petersburg's governor reported one dead and five wounded. Russia struck Zaporizhzhia overnight, and Zelenskyy said attacks on energy sites in four regions left tens of thousands of families without power.

    The 'energy ceasefire' Trump announced on 11 October has not held. Kyiv counts 76 Russian attacks on its energy, oil and gas sectors since then, and Ukraine resumed strikes on Russian oil on Thursday. On Friday, a day before they were due to take effect, Trump signed an order waiving almost all of the Graham Act's measures, including tariffs of up to 100% on major buyers of Russian oil. Zelenskyy called it a gift to Putin, and lawmakers from both parties objected.

    Why it matters. Washington's main lever on Moscow's oil revenue has been set aside with no end date, while both sides escalate against each other's energy systems going into winter. Diplomacy is weaker, and the energy war stronger, than a week ago.
