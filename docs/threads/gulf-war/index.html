Iran war and the Gulf

The US–Iran war has run since late February, with a spring ceasefire that collapsed in July. Hormuz shipping is contested but recovering, Iran's offer to reopen the strait awaits a US answer, and the Houthis are hitting both Saudi Arabia and Yemen's government.

DevelopingIntensifyingas of 19 Oct 2026

28 February 2026. 2 updates in The Signal.

  1. Monday 19 October 2026
    DevelopingIntensifying

    Houthi missiles kill at least 18 soldiers at a Yemeni army camp

    Monday's strike on a government training camp in Shabwa came as Riyadh's airport reopened and Brent slipped toward $108.

    Three ballistic missiles hit soldiers during morning training at the camp in southern Yemen, government sources told Reuters, putting the toll at 18; two military officials told AP at least 20 died. The Houthis have not commented. Government forces began an offensive this month and have gained ground near the Bab al-Mandeb strait. On Sunday, Gulf Cooperation Council ministers declared an attack on any member an attack on all, and a Houthi projectile injured six people in Najran, according to CBS. Riyadh's King Khalid airport reopened on Sunday, but many foreign airlines have kept flights suspended into late October or longer.

    Oil eased: Brent traded near $108 early on Monday, down about 2%, after the US paused its Russia sanctions and Energy Secretary Chris Wright said Gulf crude shipments are back to about four-fifths of pre-war levels. Iran's foreign minister says Tehran's offer to reopen Hormuz within six days of a deal still stands; no new US reply has been reported.

    Why it matters. The Yemen front is now a full civil war again, with the Houthis hitting the government at home as well as Saudi Arabia. Oil markets, for now, are weighing that against signs that Gulf exports are recovering.

  2. Sunday 18 October 2026
    DevelopingIntensifying

    Oil nears $110 as the Saudi–Houthi war widens

    Brent settled at $109.86 on Friday, its highest since May. Houthi missiles and drones keep hitting Saudi Arabia, and Riyadh's main airport is still closed.

    • Update, 5:58pm CT The six-nation Gulf Cooperation Council said an attack on any member is an attack on all and urged countries to designate the Houthis a terrorist organization; the Saudi-led coalition said it downed drones near Riyadh and in the Eastern Province early on Sunday. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araqchi, said Tehran's offer to reopen Hormuz within six days still stands and that it is reviewing Washington's reply.
      Sources (2)

    Since a Houthi strike on Riyadh's King Khalid airport on 10 October killed at least 12 people, Saudi-led forces have been intercepting near-daily drone and missile salvos aimed at Riyadh, the Eastern Province and Yanbu, and hitting launchers inside Yemen. Trump has said the US may join the fight; Egypt says it would defend Saudi Arabia if asked.

    The wider US–Iran war, now in its eighth month, has no working ceasefire, and talks stalled after Tehran called the latest US offer insufficient. Accounts of the Strait of Hormuz conflict: Iran's Revolutionary Guards say the strait is closed and that tankers have hit mines, while US officials say escorted routes are open and trade data show volumes recovering. Trump has said the US will not attack Iran before the 3 November midterms.

    Why it matters. Crude is up more than 75% this year, and Eurasia Group has raised its odds of a wider conflict to 65% from 35%. Fuel prices are now a live political issue in the US, Europe and Asia.
