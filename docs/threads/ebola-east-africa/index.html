Ebola in East Africa

A Bundibugyo-strain outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with no approved vaccine or treatment, has passed 9,400 cases and reached an eighth province. Kenya has eight cases, all linked to one imported patient, with no community spread so far.

DevelopingIntensifyingas of 19 Oct 2026

  1. Monday 19 October 2026
    DevelopingIntensifying

    Congo's Ebola outbreak reaches an eighth province

    Two cases, one fatal, in Nord-Ubangi take the outbreak past 9,400 cases and 4,500 deaths. Kenya's eighth case was already in quarantine.

    The Congo outbreak now covers 66 health zones, with about three-quarters of confirmed cases in Ituri, AP reports. The WHO's Africa director, Mohamed Janabi, said pockets of infection are generally declining but the disease is spreading geographically. Uganda has declared itself Ebola-free.

    In Kenya, the eighth case is a 49-year-old attendant who cared for the first patient and tested positive in quarantine; 38 people were tested and only he was positive, health officials said. 128 of 130 contacts remain in quarantine, and officials still see no community transmission.

    Why it matters. Fewer hotspots but a wider map means more borders and supply routes exposed. Kenya's cluster still looks contained, which is what keeps this regional rather than continental.

  2. Sunday 18 October 2026
    Major shiftIntensifying

    Ebola is now spreading inside Kenya

    Three health workers and a relative of Kenya's first patient have tested positive, bringing the total to seven. They are the first infections passed on within the country.

    • Update, 5:58pm CT Kenya confirmed an eighth case: a 49-year-old attendant who cared for the first patient and tested positive in quarantine after developing symptoms. Health officials say there is still no community transmission and that the other six living patients are stable.
      Sources (2)
      • Ebola cases in Kenya rise to 8 after another person tests positiveAP via KIRO 7

    The first patient had been ill in the Democratic Republic of Congo for a month before flying to Nairobi on 3 October; he died two days later. Health officials say 128 of his 130 known contacts are in quarantine and that there is no sign of community spread.

    The strain is Bundibugyo, for which there is no approved vaccine or treatment; some candidate vaccines are in early human trials. Congo's outbreak, detected in May, has passed 9,000 cases and 4,300 deaths.

    Why it matters. Infections among the doctors and nurses who treated the first patient are an early warning that hospital infection control is under strain, in a regional travel hub, with no vaccine to fall back on.
