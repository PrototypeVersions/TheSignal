Ebola in East Africa

A Bundibugyo-strain outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with no approved vaccine or treatment, has now produced local transmission in Kenya.

    Major shiftIntensifying

    Ebola is now spreading inside Kenya

    Three health workers and a relative of Kenya's first patient have tested positive, bringing the total to seven. They are the first infections passed on within the country.

    The first patient had been ill in the Democratic Republic of Congo for a month before flying to Nairobi on 3 October; he died two days later. Health officials say 128 of his 130 known contacts are in quarantine and that there is no sign of community spread.

    The strain is Bundibugyo, for which there is no approved vaccine or treatment; some candidate vaccines are in early human trials. Congo's outbreak, detected in May, has passed 9,000 cases and 4,300 deaths.

    Why it matters. Infections among the doctors and nurses who treated the first patient are an early warning that hospital infection control is under strain, in a regional travel hub, with no vaccine to fall back on.
